# Italy — Macerata

Macerata is a hill town in the Marche region, in central Italy, and the capital of
its province. With about 40,000 inhabitants, it is a small, elegant university city
with a long cultural tradition.

## A short history

| Period | What happened |
| --- | --- |
| Antiquity | The nearby Roman town of Helvia Recina declines; according to tradition, its survivors settle on the hill |
| 12th century | Macerata grows into a free commune |
| 1290 | The University of Macerata is founded, one of the oldest in Italy |
| 1500s | The city walls and many of its fine palaces are built |
| 1552 | Matteo Ricci is born here, the Jesuit who later became a scholar and missionary in China |
| 1820s | The Sferisterio arena is built |
| 1860 | After centuries of papal rule, Macerata joins the new Italian state |

## Places not to miss

- **Sferisterio**: a neoclassical open-air arena built for a ball game, now the home of the summer opera festival
- **Piazza della Libertà**: the main square, with the Loggia dei Mercanti and the theatre
- **Cathedral of San Giuliano**: the city's main church
- **City walls and gates**: wander along the 16th-century walls at sunset

## A few curiosities

> Tip: summer is the best time to visit, when the Sferisterio hosts its opera season.

1. The Sferisterio was built to host *pallone col bracciale*, a traditional ball game.
2. The Macerata Opera Festival has been held every summer since the 1920s.
3. Brick is everywhere: the warm colour of the old town comes from it.
